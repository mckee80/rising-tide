Timber framing is an ancient building method that has produced some of the most enduring and beautiful structures in human history. From medieval cathedrals in Europe to colonial barns in Pennsylvania, timber frames have sheltered humanity for thousands of years.

## What is Timber Framing?

Unlike conventional "stick framing" that uses many small dimensional lumber pieces (2x4s, 2x6s) nailed together, timber framing uses large wooden beams — called timbers — joined together with traditional **mortise-and-tenon joinery** and secured with **wooden pegs**. No nails, no screws, no metal fasteners in the frame itself.

This creates an exposed structural skeleton that is both the support system and a stunning design feature of the building.

## Why Choose Timber Framing?

### Strength & Durability
Timber frames are among the most structurally sound building methods ever devised. There are timber frame buildings standing in Europe that are over 800 years old. When properly maintained, a timber frame can last indefinitely.

### Beauty
The exposed timbers create a warm, inviting atmosphere that simply cannot be replicated with conventional construction. Whether you prefer rustic charm or contemporary elegance, the natural beauty of wood adds character and warmth to any space.

### Open Floor Plans
Because the structural support comes from the heavy timber frame, interior load-bearing walls are unnecessary. This allows for expansive, flexible floor plans with soaring ceilings and open living spaces.

### Energy Efficiency
When paired with Structural Insulated Panels (SIPs), timber frame structures are among the most energy-efficient building styles available. SIPs virtually eliminate thermal bridging, making timber frame homes nearly twice as efficient as conventional stick-built construction.

### Sustainability
Timber framing uses up to 30% less wood than conventional construction. Wood is a renewable resource, and the long lifespan of timber frame structures means less waste and rebuilding over time.
